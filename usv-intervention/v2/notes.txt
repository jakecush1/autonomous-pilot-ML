Overview:
OOR pilots a USV in replacement of Buoy 46012 which reports marine weather data to NOAA, on contract with AtOrchard.  The constraints are that the USV must stay with a 10nm radius of the Buoys former position (37.353612, -122.879874).  The USV has a pilot watchkeeping 24/7 either by OOR employees, or by our contracted pilots from Celestial.  The cost of watchkeeping is significant, but the labor is light – pilots usually allow the USV to drift, or send a command every few hours.  

The decision of when and what to send as a navigational command are mainly affected by the two things: USV location - in relation to the op zone, and the traffic lanes (we steer clear), and Wind speed and direction.  Though currents could also be an important factor, we are luckily in a zone of relatively weak currents, nor do we accurately (or at all) measure currents.  Additionally, we have observed wind to be the more influential factor of causing drift.

A notification system, if thoroughly tested and proven to be adequate and safe, could potentially change the role from a paid 24/7 watchkeeper, to an alert system with an on-call watchkeeper.

Goals: 
Develop a robust, risk analysis program which consider environmental factors affecting the USV and suggest if the USV is safe to continue drifting, or recommends human intervention should be made in the next t time period.  

Develop a notification system which runs 24/7 to reduce the effort needed to watch keep USVs station-keeping station 46012 on the NOAA project.

Notify the watchkeeper on shift if and when human intervention should be taken to reposition the USV.  

Learn about different common Machine Learning concepts.

Methods:
V1: Location Checker - Complete

Checks whether the USV’s current position needs intervention. It takes the USV’s latitude and longitude and returns Safe or Unsafe, along with the reason. It looks only at the present position and makes no prediction.

Determines if wind is blowing towards to outside or towards the center.  Input wind direction, output “Favorable Wind” or “Unfavorable Wind”

Positions are converted to UTM and expressed as (x, y) distances in nautical miles from a fixed center point, (in our case: 37.353612, −122.879874). The UTM method is so that code is reusable in a different location.

Rules being checked for Intervention Alert:

More than 10 nm from the center

More than 6.5 nm east of the center (6.8nm is traffic lane)

More than 9 nm from the center: (Warning: close to the limit)

This model can then serve as a baseline for following more complex models to expand upon.

V2: Drift prediction - In progress

V2 predicts how the USV will drift over the next h hours and how likely it is to drift into an unfavorable zone (one that V1 flags). It uses the USV’s current position, the current wind speed and direction, and a model trained on past drift data.

Drift is how far, and in which direction, the USV moves in one hour with the motor off. A linear regression predicts the next hour’s drift:

drift(t+1) = A · wind(t) + B · drift(t) + c

Here t is the current hour. To predict the next hour, the model uses the wind and the USV’s drift over the past hour. A and B are numbers the model learns from past data; they set how strongly each of those pushes the USV. c is a constant for any steady drift the other two don’t explain, such as a current. Adding the predicted drift to the current position gives where the USV will be in one hour, and repeating this hour by hour predicts its path h hours ahead.
